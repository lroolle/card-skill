# Luhmann's Zettelkasten: how it actually worked

Read 2026-10-09. Sources: Niklas Luhmann-Archiv (Bielefeld) web edition, its API and tutorial; Luhmann's 1968 talk notes (Ms. 2906) and 1981 essay (English translation); Johannes F. K. Schmidt's papers (2013/14, 2015, 2016, 2018); DHd 2018 abstract of the edition team; Treml 1990 visit report; Kieserling 2018 interview.

Marking: [fact] = stated in the linked source. [secondary] = blog or summary, weaker. [inf] = our inference.
Not done: we did not read the 1987 interview "Biographie, Attitüden, Zettelkasten" (Archimedes und wir, pp. 125-155) or the Hagen interviews directly. The only open copy of the interview excerpt (uni-siegen.de) is gone and the Wayback Machine was offline. We cite the interview only through Schmidt.

## Verdict

- [inf] The box is storage by address. Every card has one fixed home. The address is its identity and its location. Cards never move. A wrong placement is fixed with a link, not by moving the card (Q1). A card UI should give each card one stable home address.
- [inf] There are two kinds of relation, and they need different UI. Position: "continue this card" or "branch from this word". Reference: a far link written on the card by hand. Luhmann added backlinks only sometimes, by hand (Q2). Automatic backlinks are an upgrade over his practice, not a copy of it.
- [inf] You entered the box through a sparse index. A keyword pointed to 1-4 entry cards, then you walked the links (Q3). Search should land you on a few entry or hub cards, not list every match.
- [inf] "Desk = work surface" fits the evidence only in a weak form. Luhmann's 1968 rule was: take a card out when you use it, then put it back in exactly the same place. The box was built to be reached from the desk. A 1990 photo shows a box of slips on his desk (Q4). No source we found says that he laid cards out in a spatial arrangement to write. A desk as a temporary working set that returns cards home is consistent with the evidence. A persistent 2D canvas is our design choice, not Luhmann's method.
- [inf] Texts were not copied from cards. Outlines lived on cards (about 600 cards of publication plans with tables of contents) (Q4). An outline card that is a list of addresses is a native object of this method.
- [fact] The edition team itself says that its digital version makes the box available "in einer Weise ..., wie Luhmann selbst ihn nie genutzt hat" ("in a way Luhmann himself never used it"). https://zenodo.org/records/4622395 . Kieserling warns that Luhmann's own description may be "eine starke Idealisierung" ("a strong idealization"). https://publikationen.klassik-stiftung.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/ksw_derivate_00000306/08_Andr%C3%A9%20Kieserling%20%C3%BCber%20Niklas%20Luhmann_Supralibros_2018_21.pdf

## 1. Addresses and placement

- [fact] Luhmann, 1981: there is no order by topic. Each note gets a fixed number. You "place it so that it's easy to see (top left for us), and never change it". Branching example: 57/12 continues as 57/13, or branches from a word as 57/12a, then 57/12b, then 57/12a1. "In the note itself, I use red letters or numbers to mark the place of connection." (English translation) https://zettelkasten.de/communications-with-zettelkastens/ ; original: Luhmann 1981, pp. 222-228, https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-322-87749-9_19
- [fact] Luhmann, 1968 talk notes: "Fester Standort eines jeden Zettels, der nie verändert werden darf, weil davon das Finden abhängt." ("Each card has a fixed place that must never change, because finding depends on it.") New cards: "Anschliessen oder Zwischenschieben" ("append or insert"). Numbers and letters alternate "zu rascherem Erkennen" ("for quicker recognition"), for example 533/15d17a1. https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/bestand/manuskripte/manuskript/MS_2906_0001
- [fact] The same notes: "Fehlplacierungen müssen durch Verweisung korrigiert werden, nicht durch Umstellen." ("Misplacements are corrected by a reference, not by moving the card.") Same URL.
- [fact] The first number is the section. ZK I uses a comma (1,1). ZK II uses a slash (1/1). A later card that follows one point on 1,1 becomes 1,1a and goes between 1,1 and 1,2. 1,1a1 goes between 1,1a and 1,1b. https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/nachlass/zettelkasten
- [fact] Numbers grow to 13 characters, for example 21/3a1p5c4fB1a (first card of "Confidentiality"). https://sociologica.unibo.it/article/view/8350 . Between two cards that were first written in sequence, later insertions can reach several hundred cards (https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/nachlass/zettelkasten) or up to about 1,000 (https://zenodo.org/records/4622395).
- [fact] The rule is not strict. "The number on its own does not really inform about the relations between different notes." Alternation was sometimes broken. A plain 1, 1a, 1b chain on one topic also occurs. https://sociologica.unibo.it/article/view/8350 (note 12)
- [fact] Where a card goes is a local decision. A card only has to connect to the card before it. "There are no privileged positions ... there is no top and no bottom." The position says nothing about the theoretical weight of the card. Same URL. Example: most cards on autopoiesis sit at the minor-looking address 21/3d26g1i. https://www.uni-bielefeld.de/fakultaeten/soziologie/forschung/luhmann-archiv/pdf/jschmidt_zettelkasten-als-uberraschungsgenerator.pdf
- [fact] Luhmann, 1981: "With this technique it is not important where you place a new note." When there are several options, put it anywhere and add references for the others. https://zettelkasten.de/communications-with-zettelkastens/
- [fact] Numbers are not always unique. ZK II has two cards numbered 1. The edition shows accidental duplicates as "parallel" strands. https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/bestand/zettelkasten/tutorial ; https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/bestand/zettelkasten/zettel/ZK_2_NB_1_2_V
- [fact] Cards carry no date. Only the handwriting dates them. https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/nachlass/zettelkasten
- [inf] The archive uses "Folgezettel" only for a card that continues a text. The community uses the word for the whole branching address scheme. Luhmann's own words were "anschließen" and "zwischenschieben" (1968) and "innere Verzweigungsfähigkeit" (1981, "capacity for internal branching").

## 2. Cross-references (Verweisungen)

- [fact] Yes, he wrote the target number on the card. "Luhmann on one card noted a number of another card, often thematically and spatially far away." https://sociologica.unibo.it/article/view/8350
- [fact] Counts (sample-based estimates): ZK I about 18,000-20,000 references, ZK II about 25,000-28,000. Very few links go between ZK I and ZK II. https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/nachlass/zettelkasten . Schmidt 2018 gives about 20,000 and 30,000: "a reference link on nearly every (first collection) or nearly every second note (second collection)". https://sociologica.unibo.it/article/view/8350 . The edition team gives about 50,000 in total. https://zenodo.org/records/4622395
- [fact] Types, per the archive: (a1) near reference to a side thought on the next cards, marked by a red digit or lower-case letter that also appears on the target; (a2) outline reference, a red capital letter pointing to a nearby card; (a3) far reference to another part of the box, often inline at a single term; (b) collective reference card at the start of a block, with up to 25 numbers, usually each with its keyword. https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/nachlass/zettelkasten
- [fact] Backlinks were manual and partial: on the target card "findet man häufiger auch einen Rückverweis" ("one often also finds a reference back"), which the archive calls a bidirectional reference. In ZK I references are mostly in pencil. Same URL.
- [fact] Collective reference cards were extended over time. The handwriting and pens differ. Same URL. Luhmann kept updating old cards when new cards were filed elsewhere. https://sociologica.unibo.it/article/view/8350
- [fact] Concrete example: ZK II card 1 reads "Verweisungen: Organisationszweck 532/4b ... Evtl. 5/5 als Grundgedanke". https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/bestand/zettelkasten/zettel/ZK_2_NB_1_1_V
- [fact] Unlinked cards are lost. "A note that is not connected to this network will get lost in the Zettelkasten." https://zettelkasten.de/communications-with-zettelkastens/
- [inf] Our own count, from the edition API on 2026-10-09: the first 1,252 transcribed cards of ZK II drawer 1 (1 to 21/3a1p4B6) carry 672 far references, 437 near references and 47 outline references. 29% of these cards have at least one far link. The top card (21/3a1p4A) has 17 far links. This is one early section, so it is not representative of the whole box. https://v0.api.niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/ZK/2/auszug/01?page=1

## 3. The register (keyword index)

- [fact] ZK I: about 1,250 keywords, handwritten on 73 slips, only roughly alphabetical. ZK II: four successive versions. The last one, from the 1990s, has about 3,200 entries typed on 244 normal index cards, fully alphabetical. https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/nachlass/zettelkasten
- [fact] Each keyword lists usually at most four card numbers. Luhmann did not try to list every relevant card, because the reference network leads on from the entry points. Same URL. Schmidt: "typically ... only one to four places". https://sociologica.unibo.it/article/view/8350
- [fact] Luhmann, 1968: two finding aids are enough for him, "1) alphabetisches Stichwortverzeichnis; 2) Notizen auf den Literaturzetteln" ("an alphabetical keyword index; notes on the bibliography slips"). https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/bestand/manuskripte/manuskript/MS_2906_0001
- [fact] Other entry points: a person index of about 300 names (at most 3 places each). ZK II has about 15,000 bibliography slips. Luhmann wrote card numbers on them so they could serve as entry points. https://www.uni-bielefeld.de/fakultaeten/soziologie/forschung/luhmann-archiv/pdf/jschmidt-aus-dem-archiv.pdf
- [fact] There was no table of contents for ZK II. Only ZK I has a flat list of its 108 sections. Same URL.

## 4. Working with cards outside the box

What we found, from strongest to weakest:
- [fact] Luhmann, 1968: "Herausnehmen bei Benutzung und exakt Wiedereinstellen." ("Take it out when you use it, and put it back in exactly the same place.") This is the only primary statement we found that cards left the box during use. https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/bestand/manuskripte/manuskript/MS_2906_0001
- [fact] Luhmann, 1981: write on the front side only, "so it is possible to read the note during searches without the need to take it out". Use thin paper, because after decades the box "might become impossible to access ... from your place at the desk". https://zettelkasten.de/communications-with-zettelkastens/ . The archive repeats this: cards were written on one side so they could be read "ohne sie aus dem jeweiligen Auszug herausnehmen zu müssen" ("without taking them out of the drawer"). https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/nachlass/zettelkasten
- [fact] Schmidt: the analog box "required the more time-consuming process of physically looking up and taking out the respective card instead of a simple mouse click". https://sociologica.unibo.it/article/view/8350
- [fact] Schmidt reports that Luhmann (1987 interview, p. 144) described writing from the box as "a kind of collaging technique": he combined the sections on functional differentiation, self-referential systems and binarity for one talk. Schmidt adds that the text came "nowhere near" the complexity of those sections, and some cards were written during the work. https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/download/2942475/2942530/jschmidt_2016_niklas%20luhmanns%20card%20index.pdf (pp. 310-311)
- [fact] Luhmann, 1981: "Scientific publications thus don't emerge ... by copying the note content that was intended for publishing." https://zettelkasten.de/communications-with-zettelkastens/
- [fact] Drawer 17 of ZK II holds about 600 cards with publication plans, with titles and partly detailed tables of contents. Some card backs hold "einzelpublikationsbezogene Zettelsammlungen" (card sets for single publications). https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/nachlass/zettelkasten
- [fact] Treml's 1990 visit report prints a photo captioned "Ein Blick auf Luhmanns Schreibtisch. Stilleben mit Zettelkasten." ("A view of Luhmann's desk. Still life with card box.") https://doi.org/10.25656/01:23858 (p. 36). [inf] In the photo an open box full of slips stands on the desk, among loose papers and a teapot. It does not show cards laid out in an arrangement.
- [fact] Not found: any source that says Luhmann spread cards on a desk, table or floor to compose a text or to think. We checked the 1968 notes, the 1981 essay, the archive description and tutorial, Schmidt 2013/14, 2015, 2016 and 2018, the edition's DHd 2018 abstract and a full-text search of the edition for "Schreibtisch" (5 hits, none about method). The 1987 interview and the Hagen interviews remain unread. Treat "he laid his cards out on the desk" as unverified.

## 5. Physical facts

- [fact] About 90,000 undated cards, almost all handwritten, in two mostly separate collections. https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/nachlass/zettelkasten
- [fact] ZK I: about 22,000 cards, about 1952-1960 (some additions up to about 1973), 108 sections, 7 drawers. ZK II: about 67,000 cards, 1961 to early 1997, 11 top sections, 20 drawers (17 in the wooden cabinets plus 3 external boxes). Same URL. Schmidt 2018 gives ZK I as about 23,000 cards from about 1951-1962, and ZK II from 1963-1997 (https://sociologica.unibo.it/article/view/8350). Schmidt 2016 gives ZK II 21 drawers (https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/download/2942475/2942530/jschmidt_2016_niklas%20luhmanns%20card%20index.pdf). We use the archive's current figures.
- [fact] At his death in 1998: 27 drawers. These were six beech-wood cabinets with four drawers each, plus three separate boxes of reinforced cardboard for bibliography slips. The drawers had no outside labels. Each drawer holds 2,500-3,500 slips. https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/nachlass/zettelkasten . (Schmidt 2016 says four cardboard boxes; Schmidt 2015 says two.)
- [fact] Paper: DIN A6 (105 x 148 mm). He used thin paper, not index cards, often cut from used A4 sheets, also calendar sheets and bank statements. https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/nachlass/zettelkasten . In 1968 he said a portable cardboard box is enough at first, "später evtl. Holzkästen in Viererblocks" ("later perhaps wooden boxes in blocks of four"), and "Papierzettel (oktav) genügen vollauf" ("octavo paper slips are fully sufficient"). https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/bestand/manuskripte/manuskript/MS_2906_0001
- [fact] From the mid-1970s the box stood in his study in his house in Oerlinghausen. The order of the drawers at his death cannot be reconstructed, because nobody documented it during removal. https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/nachlass/zettelkasten

## 6. What the digital edition shows about links

- [fact] Far references are clickable links in the transcription, shown in blue. Near references stay red, as in the original. https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/bestand/zettelkasten/tutorial
- [fact] There are three ways to browse: physical order in the drawer, Luhmann's numbering, and "logical" strands that the editors built (continue, inserted strand, complementary strand, parallel strand, back to the higher strand). Same URL.
- [fact] Below each card there is a local visualization of the card sequence. Cards are circles. Branch types have colours: red complementary, pink inserted, green parallel, yellow back side. It shows the placement of the current card, not the whole box. Same URL.
- [fact] This view draws the branch tree only. Its data files contain next, previous and sub-branch pointers, and no cross-references. https://assets.niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/zettel-branch-vis-data/2/01/01/ZK_2_01_01_003_V_N_NB_1_2.json
- [fact] The edition team also made an arc diagram of the internal references in drawer 1 of ZK I (about 3,300 cards). It shows clear differences in link density inside and between its 21 sections. https://zenodo.org/records/4622395 (Fig. 4). [inf] We did not find this arc diagram in the public portal's code. It may exist only in the paper or in internal tools.
- [fact] Each bibliography entry lists all cards that cite the source ("Fundstellen im Portalbestand"). https://niklas-luhmann-archiv.de/bestand/zettelkasten/tutorial
- [inf] No global link graph is public. The edition presents the box as one card plus its local branch neighbourhood plus inline links. That matches how Luhmann read it: one card at a time, then follow a number.
