* Org: the board format

** Boards are plain Org, with nothing invented on top of it
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: org-format
:BASIS: fact
:END:
A section is a ~*~ heading, and a card is a ~**~ heading. A property drawer holds the id and the links. The TODO keyword is the status. The markdown dialect needed its own ~{...}~ attributes; Org needs none.

- card :: ~** The claim~, then a ~:PROPERTIES:~ drawer (was ~## The claim {#id}~)
- status :: the TODO keyword: ~DOING~, ~BLOCKED~, ~DONE~ (was ~status=done~)
- progress :: a cookie at the end of the claim, ~[3/8]~ (was ~progress=3/8~)
- facts :: a description list, ~- key :: value~ (was a facts fence)
- figure :: ~#+caption:~, then ~#+begin_src sketch~ or ~flow~
- link :: ~[[#id]]~ (was ~[[id]]~)

The spec is =skill/reference/format.md=. The board reads correctly in Emacs, on GitHub, and in any editor. An older =board.md= still renders.

** Converting a board to Org is not a revision; a change in meaning is
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: org-meaning
:BASIS: fact
:FROM: org-format
:END:
The compiler compares what a card means: its claim, its links, and its rendered body. This board went from markdown to Org, and the cards that did not change kept their versions. A re-wrapped paragraph is not a new version either.

#+caption: What decides a new version
#+begin_src flow
  card in board.md -> meaning: parse, render
  card in board.org -> meaning: parse, render
  meaning -> same version: equal
  meaning --> new version: different
#+end_src

** Luhmann never moved a card: he took cards out to use them and put each one back
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: luhmann
:BASIS: fact
:END:
Each card had a fixed address, because finding a card depended on it. A card in the wrong place got a link, not a new place. He took a card out to use it and put it back in the same place. We found no source that he spread cards on a desk to write.

   └────────┘  └───────┘
#+end_src

- cards :: about 90,000, in two collections
- address :: fixed; 57/12 goes on as 57/13 or branches as 57/12a
- wrong place :: fixed by a link, never by a move
- far links :: about 50,000, written by hand on the cards
- way in :: a short index, 1 to 4 cards for each keyword

What this means for the board:

- The rack is the box. Each card keeps one numeral and one place in board.org.
- The desk is the work. It shows the same cards and moves none of them in board.org.
- His far links are our ~from=~, ~needs=~ and =[[id]]=. He walked them by hand. The desk draws them.
- His editors drew an arc diagram of the links in one drawer to see where links are dense. The arcs in a desk column are the same kind of drawing.

The picture of Luhmann with cards spread across a desk is not in the sources we read. The closest source is his 1968 rule: "take it out when you use it, and put it back in exactly the same place". A 1987 interview, through Johannes Schmidt, calls his writing for one talk "a kind of collaging technique". So the desk is our design, not his method. The 1987 interview itself is unread. ** A flow block draws boxes and arrows, and the compiler places them
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: fig-flow
:BASIS: fact
:FROM: show-first
:END:
Write one arrow on each line, such as =a -> b: label=. The compiler puts the boxes in ranks, orders them to cut crossings, and draws the arrows. The agent never places a box, so the same text always gives the same picture.

#+caption: The card-skill loop
#+begin_src flow
  *board.org* -> cards render: agent writes
  cards render -> board.html
  board.html -> you: open
  you -> log.jsonl: Send
  log.jsonl -> agent: cards wait
  agent -> *board.org*: revise
#+end_src

- =a -> b= is an arrow. =a --> b= is a dashed arrow, for a later or optional step.
- =a -> b: label= puts a label on the arrow. =a -> b -> c= is a chain.
- =*a*= makes a bold box, for the part under discussion.
- =direction: right= draws left to right. The default is down.
- A flow has 12 boxes at most. Point at a box to darken its arrows.

:PROPERTIES:

:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: unit-of-judgment
